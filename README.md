Why people use a [Bitcoin mixers](https://bitcoin-mixers-list.com/) at all

Here's the thing a lot of newcomers miss: Bitcoin isn't anonymous. Every payment sits on a public ledger forever. The moment someone ties one of your addresses to your name — through an exchange withdrawal, a shop delivery, a donation page — they can usually follow the money backwards and forwards for years.

That's uncomfortable even if you've done nothing wrong. A freelancer doesn't want every client seeing what the others paid. Someone who got paid in BTC doesn't want the buyer of their used laptop to see their entire balance. Activists, journalists and people living under unpredictable governments have sharper reasons still.

Mixing services exist to cut that thread. You send coins in, the service shuffles them with coins from other users, and you get back coins with no obvious history connecting them to what you sent. That's the whole idea. The details are where it gets interesting — and where most of the risk hides.

How a [BTC mixer](https://bitcoin-mixers-list.com/) actually works

1) You give it a destination
   
You paste one or more fresh addresses where you want clean coins to land. Good services let you split the payout across several addresses and set a different delay for each.

2) It gives you a deposit address
   
Along with it you should get a letter of guarantee — a message signed by the service's key that proves it promised to pay you. Save it. If something goes wrong, it's your only receipt.

3) Your coins join a pool
   
Your deposit sits in a reserve alongside everybody else's. Nothing you sent is ever sent back to you; the coins you receive come from a completely different part of the pool.

4) Different coins come back
   
After the delay, minus the fee, the service pays out to your addresses. On-chain, there's no transaction linking your deposit to your withdrawal — only the service's logs could connect them.
